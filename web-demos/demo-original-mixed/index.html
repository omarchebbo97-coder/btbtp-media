From the introduction

Introduction

Being different is a quiet kind of loneliness. It’s not just about standing out, it’s about existing in a world that was never designed for you. You think you’re living your life, but in reality, that thing, the illness, is your life. While others move effortlessly, making plans, chasing dreams, taking health for granted, you are tethered to something invisible yet inescapable.

A chronic illness isn’t a bad day or a temporary setback. It becomes the blueprint of your life. It doesn’t wait for you to be ready. It doesn’t ask for permission. It demands adaptation, whether you’re willing or not.

For most people, the body is a silent companion, breathing, repairing, digesting without question. For me, the body is a battlefield, covered in scars and stitched together from strangers’ generosity. My liver? Not mine. My kidney? Not mine. My body is a collaboration of surgeries, medications, and borrowed parts. How do you even begin to make peace with something like that?

When your body fails you early on, life doesn’t unfold like it does for others. You don’t plan careers or adventures. You measure time in blood results, in medication alarms, in hospital corridors. You carry an exhaustion no sleep can fix, and an awareness most people never have to face: the fragility of simply being alive.

Questions

Before you ask

Is this book medical advice?

No. I'm not a doctor or therapist. The book shares my lived experience and practical field notes. Always talk to your own care team before acting on anything you read here.

Who is this book for?

Patients on dialysis or awaiting transplant, recipients rebuilding their lives, the caregivers beside them, and anyone who loves an honest memoir about survival and identity.

Do I need medical knowledge to follow it?

Not at all. The story carries you; medical moments are explained in plain words, as I understood them from the patient's chair.

How do I get the book after paying?

The introduction comes by email right after payment, as a PDF. The full memoir comes out in June 2027: the ebook by email, the hardcover delivered free. All sales are final.

How do I pay?

With Whish. At checkout you go to Whish to confirm the payment with your Whish account, then you come straight back here for your receipt.

How do I know the money reaches AUBMC?

I've already made the first donation to the AUBMC dialysis unit. The hospital's signed confirmation will be published here, and each new donation will be added the same way.

Can I invite Omar to speak or interview?

Sure! Write to me at hello@omarchebbo.com or use the contact page ↗, and tell me about your event, podcast or publication. I read everything personally.
